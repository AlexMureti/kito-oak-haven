Try the middle button. A guest arriving on the 10th and your guest leaving on the 10th share a date, but not a night — so that stay is free to take. A system that gets this wrong quietly turns away a real booking every time a stay ends.

How it knows

Airbnb publishes every listing's booked nights as a calendar link. It is generated once and keeps working — you never open it again.

A program reads that link every three hours and writes your booked nights into the same place as the website bookings. Three hours is not a guess: Airbnb rebuilds its own calendar file on a three-hour cycle, so checking more often reads the same thing twice.

Both channels then sit in one view, and are these nights free? gets answered by looking, instead of by someone remembering to ask.

What you would do

Once. Then never again.

I

Send one link

In Airbnb: Calendar → Availability → Connect calendars → Export calendar. Copy what it gives you and send it over. That is the entire setup, and it takes about thirty seconds.

II

Nothing else

Your team keeps working exactly as they do now. Nothing changes on their side and nobody has to report a booking to anyone.

III

Look whenever you want

You get view access to the calendar. Ninety nights ahead, colour-coded exactly like the strip above.

The rules it follows

An enquiry does not hold a night. Only a confirmed booking blocks dates — otherwise every person who ever tapped the WhatsApp button would be holding nights they were never going to take.

A calendar that fails to load is treated as a failure, not as an empty calendar. Airbnb's export sometimes returns an error page instead of your dates. A careless version reads that as "everything was cancelled" and releases every night you have sold. This one changes nothing and records a warning.

Nothing is ever deleted. A booking that disappears is marked released, not removed, so there is always a record of what was held and when.

Every check is written down. Every run leaves a line, including the ones that found nothing. A system that fails silently is the only way this hurts anyone.

What it does not do

It does not confirm bookings on its own. It makes a collision visible and raises an alert — the decision stays with a person.

It also cannot see a booking your team takes outside Airbnb. If a stay is sold over the phone and blocked on Airbnb afterwards, this sees it the moment they do. If it is never blocked there, nothing can see it. Worth knowing now rather than discovering later.
